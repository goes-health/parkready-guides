Yellowstone National Park this week

Winter shoulder season: only the North ↔ Northeast road is open to cars.

Yellowstone closed its West, South and East entrances and nearly all roads to regular vehicles on Nov. 1. Until snowmobile and snowcoach season starts Dec. 15, the only way to drive into the park is the road between the North Entrance (Gardiner, MT) and the Northeast Entrance (Cooke City/Silver Gate, MT), through Mammoth Hot Springs, Tower Junction and Lamar Valley. Gardiner, MT

North Entrance

The North Entrance at Gardiner, Montana is the only Yellowstone entrance open to wheeled vehicles all year, and right now it's one of only two ways to drive in. From here the road climbs to Mammoth Hot Springs and continues east through Tower Junction and Lamar Valley to the Northeast Entrance.

Open to cars (year-round road)

Northeast Entrance

The Northeast Entrance near Silver Gate and Cooke City, Montana sits at the far end of Yellowstone's only winter road. With the Beartooth Highway closed for the season, the way to Cooke City in winter is through the park from Gardiner and Lamar Valley.

Know the risks

What matters in Yellowstone right now

Teach, don't scare: these are the hazards NPS emphasizes this season, with what to do.

Thermal areas (scald burns)

Burns from thermal features are a common cause of serious injury and death in Yellowstone. Ground that looks solid can be a thin crust over superheated water. Stay on boardwalks, keep kids close, and never touch runoff. More than 20 people have died after entering or falling into hot springs. Biscuit Basin (near Old Faithful) remains closed after the July 2024 hydrothermal explosion. NPSNPS alert

Bears

All of Yellowstone is grizzly and black bear country. Stay at least 100 yards from bears and wolves, carry bear spray where you can reach it, hike in groups of 3+, make noise, and avoid carcasses. Since 1970, 91% of people injured by bears in Yellowstone were hiking alone or with one partner. NPS

In GOESWildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for bears and other dangerous animals

Bison, elk and other wildlife

Stay at least 25 yards from bison, elk and all other animals (100 yards from bears, wolves and cougars). The safest view is usually from inside your car. NPS

In GOESWildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for dangerous animals

Winter driving and cold

NPS asks fall and winter drivers to watch the weather, keep plans flexible, expect limited services and closures without notice, pack blankets and cold-weather essentials, and keep a full tank. NPS news
