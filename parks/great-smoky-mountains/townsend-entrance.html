Know the risks

What actually matters here in mid-November

The goal is a good trip, not a scary one. These are the hazards NPS highlights for this area and season, and what to do about each.

Bear jams in Cades Cove

Cades Cove is one of the park's best places to see bears, and one of the easiest places to get too close. Approaching within 50 yards is illegal. Use pull-offs and don't stop in the road. NPS newsNPS

Park-wide

Great Smoky Mountains risks everywhere in the park

Black bears

Over the last 10 years the Smokies averaged 339 reported negative human–bear encounters a year. In fall, bears feed heavily (up to 20,000 calories a day) and show up near roadsides. Approaching within 50 yards (150 ft) is illegal. If a black bear attacks and isn't after your food, fight back; don't play dead. NPSNPS news

In GOESWildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for bears and other dangerous animals

Elk

It's illegal to approach elk within 25 yards (75 ft). During the fall rut, bulls can be aggressive toward people, vehicles and pets. NPS closed the fields around Oconaluftee and Cataloochee to visitors during the rut, so obey any posted closures. NPS newsNPS news

In GOESWildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for dangerous animals

Wet cold and fast-changing mountain weather

Temperatures can vary 10–20°F between base and summit, and clear skies lower down don't guarantee the same up high. Rain, then snow, then near-freezing nights is a textbook setup for hypothermia. NPS APINWS

GPS and no signal

NPS warns that GPS units may send drivers the wrong way on one-way roads or to dead ends, and points mobile users to offline maps for areas without cell service. NPS
