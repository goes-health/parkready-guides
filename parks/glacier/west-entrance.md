# Glacier West Entrance: this week

> Week of Nov 9–15, 2026 · Glacier National Park · West Glacier, MT (US-2) · Status: **Open — road to Avalanche Creek**. Facts fetched Nov 12, 2026 from official NPS and NWS sources.

HTML version: https://parks.goes.health/parks/glacier/west-entrance.html

The West Entrance at West Glacier is the park's year-round front door to Apgar Village, Lake McDonald and the lower Going-to-the-Sun Road. It's about 33 miles from Kalispell.

## This week at the West Entrance (official conditions)

- **Open:** Going-to-the-Sun Road is open from West Glacier and closed for the season at Avalanche Creek. NPS's winter page says the road from West Glacier to Lake McDonald Lodge is plowed year-round, weather permitting. Sources: [NPS Glacier alerts (NPS Data API, fetched Nov 12)](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [Glacier: visiting in winter](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/winter.htm)
- **Info:** Beyond the closure gate, the unplowed road is open to hikers and bicyclists, and to skiing and snowshoeing once there's snow. Sources: [Glacier operating hours & seasons](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/hours.htm); [Glacier: visiting in winter](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/winter.htm)
- **Info:** Apgar Visitor Center restrooms are open year-round, with a water bottle filling station (about 2 miles north of the entrance). NPS's winter page says the visitor center opens on weekends in winter. Sources: [Glacier operating hours & seasons](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/hours.htm); [Glacier: visiting in winter](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/winter.htm)
- **Info:** Apgar Campground offers primitive camping (vault toilet, no running water, first-come, first-served). There's no lodging in the park this time of year. Sources: [Glacier operating hours & seasons](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/hours.htm)
- **Info:** No fuel inside the park. Road alerts: text GNPROADS to 333111, tune to Travelers' Information Station 1610 AM, or call 406-888-7800. Sources: [Glacier directions, transportation & road status](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/directions.htm); [Glacier: visiting in winter](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/winter.htm)

**Entrance fee:** Private vehicle $35 (self-pay when stations are unstaffed). Non-US residents 16+ pay an additional $100 nonresident fee unless entering with an annual / America the Beautiful pass. Sources: [NPS Glacier fees & passes](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/fees.htm); [NPS Data API (parks, operating hours, fees)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/api-documentation.htm)

## What actually matters here this season

The goal is a good trip, not a scary one. These are the hazards NPS highlights for this area and season, and what to do about each.

- **Bears on the lower road and trails.** Bears may still be active and foraging before denning. Carry bear spray, call out and clap (NPS says bells aren't enough), and don't stop near roadside bears: drive at least 100 yards past before pulling over. Sources: [Glacier bear safety](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/bears.htm); [Glacier operating hours & seasons](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/hours.htm)
  - In GOES: Wildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for bears and other dangerous animals
- **Lake McDonald's cold water.** Water is the park's top cause of death. Slick shoreline rocks and near-freezing water make a fall dangerous within minutes. Sources: [Glacier current conditions & safety](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments
- **Fog and short days.** NWS forecast for West Glacier this week: areas of fog, highs near 50–54°F, lows near 35°F, and a slight chance of light rain Saturday. Sources: [National Weather Service point forecasts (api.weather.gov, fetched Nov 12)](https://www.weather.gov/)
  - In GOES: 125+ extreme weather alerts

## Glacier park-wide

- **Grizzly and black bears.** Glacier has habitat for nearly 1,000 bears of both species. Stay 100 yards from bears and wolves (25 yards from other wildlife), carry bear spray, make noise and hike in groups: Glacier has had no reported attacks on groups of four or more. In fall, bears forage hard before denning. Sources: [Glacier bear safety](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/bears.htm); [Glacier current conditions & safety](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [Glacier operating hours & seasons](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/hours.htm)
  - In GOES: Wildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for bears and other dangerous animals
- **Cold water.** Water is the number one cause of fatalities in Glacier. Use extreme caution near lakes and streams. Sources: [Glacier current conditions & safety](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments
- **Tularemia.** NPS confirmed tularemia in dead snowshoe hares in Many Glacier (Oct 15 release) and the St. Mary Lake area (Sept 17 release). Use insect repellent, check for ticks, don't touch carcasses, drink only treated or boiled water, and see a doctor if you get fever, chills or sores within two weeks of your visit. Sources: [News release: Tularemia detected in Many Glacier Valley (Oct 15, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/glac/learn/news/tularemia-detected-in-glacier-national-park-s-many-glacier-valley.htm); [News release: Tularemia detected in St. Mary Lake area (Sep 17, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/glac/learn/news/tularemia-detected-in-st-mary-lake-area-of-glacier-national-park.htm)
  - In GOES: Tick Risk: location- and season-specific tick activity
- **Winter self-sufficiency.** Most businesses and services in and near the park are closed from early October to late May. Expect cold, blustery days and be entirely self-sufficient. Sources: [Glacier operating hours & seasons](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/hours.htm); [Glacier current conditions & safety](https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments
