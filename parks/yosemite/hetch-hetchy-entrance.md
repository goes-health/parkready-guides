# Yosemite Hetch Hetchy Entrance: this week

> Week of Nov 23–29, 2026 · Yosemite National Park · Evergreen Road (off Hwy 120) · Status: **Daylight only — closes for snow**. Facts fetched Nov 23, 2026 from official NPS and NWS sources.

HTML version: https://parks.goes.health/parks/yosemite/hetch-hetchy-entrance.html

Hetch Hetchy is Yosemite's quiet northwest corner, a reservoir valley at about 3,900 feet reached on Evergreen and Hetch Hetchy roads off Hwy 120. NPS puts it about 1.5 hours from Yosemite Valley.

## This week at the Hetch Hetchy Entrance (official conditions)

- **Heads-up:** Hetch Hetchy Road: open sunrise to sunset only, and closed when it's snowing or snow is forecast overnight. Day visitors must leave by sunset; the road is closed to all vehicles overnight. Sources: [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [Yosemite operating hours (Hetch Hetchy daylight hours)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/hours.htm); [NPS Data API (parks, operating hours, fees)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/api-documentation.htm)
- **Open:** No current trail closures in the Hetch Hetchy area. Sources: [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
- **Info:** Swimming and boating are prohibited in the reservoir (it's a drinking water supply). Pets aren't allowed on the dam or trails. Sources: [Yosemite: Hetch Hetchy day hikes](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/hetchhetchy.htm)

**Entrance fee:** Private vehicle $35. Non-US residents 16+ pay an additional $100 nonresident fee unless entering with an annual / America the Beautiful pass. Sources: [NPS Yosemite fees & passes](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/fees.htm); [NPS Data API (parks, operating hours, fees)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/api-documentation.htm)

## What actually matters here this season

The goal is a good trip, not a scary one. These are the hazards NPS highlights for this area and season, and what to do about each.

- **Getting caught by darkness.** Sunset comes early in mid-November and the gate closes at sunset. Turn around with daylight to spare: a twisted ankle on the Wapama Falls trail can turn into a cold night out. Sources: [Yosemite operating hours (Hetch Hetchy daylight hours)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/hours.htm); [Yosemite: Hetch Hetchy day hikes](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/hetchhetchy.htm)
  - In GOES: Offline interactive health assessment + 50+ visual how-to guides
- **Rattlesnakes and poison oak.** NPS's Hetch Hetchy page tells hikers to watch for rattlesnakes and poison oak. Sources: [Yosemite: Hetch Hetchy day hikes](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/hetchhetchy.htm)
  - In GOES: Wildlife Encounters (venomous snakes) + Poisonous Plants (poison oak, ivy, sumac)
- **Frost and a late-week rain chance.** This week's NWS forecast here: widespread frost early in the week, highs around 52–68°F, lows around 30–38°F, and light rain likely Saturday night. If snow is forecast overnight, the road may not open. Sources: [National Weather Service point forecasts (api.weather.gov, fetched Nov 23)](https://www.weather.gov/); [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments

## Yosemite park-wide

- **Black bears in all areas.** NPS: bears remain active in all areas of the park. Store food properly, use bear-proof dumpsters, and stay at least 50 yards from bears in forest and meadows — but yell at and scare away a bear that approaches you or is in a developed area. Yosemite has an estimated 300–500 black bears and no grizzlies. Sources: [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [Yosemite bears](https://www.nps.gov/yose/learn/nature/bears.htm)
  - In GOES: Wildlife Encounters: location-specific risk indicators for bears and other dangerous animals
- **Rivers and slick rock.** Waterfalls are flowing low, as usual for November, but NPS still asks visitors to use caution along rivers and creeks: rocks are slippery even when dry. Sources: [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
  - In GOES: Offline interactive health assessment + 50+ visual how-to guides
- **Frosty mornings, chains and ice.** There are no NWS watches or warnings for the park this week. Days are mild (50s to mid-60s in the Valley), but NWS forecasts widespread frost near Big Oak Flat and Hetch Hetchy with lows around 28–34°F. NPS: carry chains even with 4WD and expect icy spots on mostly dry roads. Sources: [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [National Weather Service point forecasts (api.weather.gov, fetched Nov 23)](https://www.weather.gov/)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments
- **Smoke and air quality.** The Dome Fire (Sept–Oct) was 86% contained with minimal activity as of NPS's Oct 13 update, and fire closures remain around the Glacier Point Road area. Air quality can still be affected by fires elsewhere or prescribed burns, so check current AQI before strenuous hikes. Sources: [Dome Fire update, Oct 13, 2026](https://www.nps.gov/yose/learn/news/dome-fire-update-october-13-2026-morning.htm); [Yosemite Current Conditions (updated Nov 20, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [AirNow fire & smoke map](https://fire.airnow.gov/)
  - In GOES: Respiratory Hazards: smoke, pollution and pollen (AQI) monitoring

## All active Yosemite NPS alerts

From the NPS Data API, fetched Nov 23, 2026. Live: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm

- **[Park Closure] Tioga and Glacier Point Roads are closed due snow.** Tioga Road (continuation of Highway 120 through the park) and Glacier Point Road are closed for the season. They usually reopen in late May or June, depending on conditions. (https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/wroads.htm)
- **[Information] Tire chains may be required.** Carry tire chains in your car and be prepared to use them (even if your car has four-wheel drive): chains may become mandatory at any time. Drive carefully even when there are no chain requirements: you may encounter icy spots on mostly dry roads. (https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/tirechains.htm)
