# Grand Canyon South Entrance: this week

> Week of Nov 9–15, 2026 · Grand Canyon National Park · Tusayan, AZ (Hwy 64 / 180) · Status: **Open 24/7 — day use, bring water**. Facts fetched Nov 12, 2026 from official NPS and NWS sources.

HTML version: https://goes-health.github.io/parkready-guides/parks/grand-canyon/south-entrance.html

The South Entrance just north of Tusayan is the main gate into Grand Canyon's South Rim (NPS says it gets the most traffic). It leads to Grand Canyon Village, the main visitor center and the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trailheads.

## This week at the South Entrance (official conditions)

- **Open:** Open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Credit cards and America the Beautiful passes accepted; cash is not accepted. Sources: [Grand Canyon operating hours](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/hours.htm); [NPS Data API (parks, operating hours, fees)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/api-documentation.htm)
- **Heads-up:** NPS notes this station has the longest lines, with waits of up to 2 hours between 9:30 am and 4 pm. That's NPS's general guidance, not a live wait time. Desert View (East) usually has shorter waits. Sources: [NPS Data API (parks, operating hours, fees)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/api-documentation.htm)
- **Heads-up:** Overnight accommodations are suspended; only dry camping is allowed. Food and beverage services, the Grand Canyon Clinic and the Post Office remain open. Sources: [NPS Grand Canyon alerts (NPS Data API, fetched Nov 12)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/conditions.htm); [News release linked from the NPS alert: overnight accommodations suspended](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/grand-canyon-national-park-to-close-overnight-hotel-accommodations-beginning-august-31.htm)
- **Info:** Stage 2 fire restrictions were lifted on the South Rim Nov 4; Stage 1 remains. Campfires at Mather Campground are allowed only in established rings. Sources: [News release: Stage 2 fire restrictions rescinded on South Rim (Nov 4, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/stage-2-fire-restrictions-rescinded-on-south-rim.htm)
- **Open:** Open trails: Bright Angel to River Resthouse, Plateau Point, River Trail, South Kaibab to the Black Bridge, and Tonto Trail (Tip-Off to Havasupai Gardens). Expect intermittent closures for Bright Angel Trail repairs. Sources: [Grand Canyon Key Hiking Messages (updated Oct 27, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm); [News release: Select inner canyon trails reopen (Oct 26, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/select-inner-canyon-trails-reopen-in-grand-canyon-national-park.htm)

**Entrance fee:** Private vehicle $35 (cash is not accepted at the entrances). Non-US residents 16+ pay an additional $100 nonresident fee unless entering with an annual / America the Beautiful pass. Sources: [NPS Grand Canyon fees & passes](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/fees.htm); [NPS Data API (parks, operating hours, fees)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/api-documentation.htm)

## What actually matters here this season

The goal is a good trip, not a scary one. These are the hazards NPS highlights for this area and season, and what to do about each.

- **Dehydration in high, dry air.** At 7,000 feet in desert air you lose water fast, and the park is asking everyone to bring their own. Inner-canyon water stations are off. Sources: [Grand Canyon safety: elevation, rim edges, winter](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/safety.htm); [Grand Canyon Key Hiking Messages (updated Oct 27, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm)
  - In GOES: High Altitude Illness: personalized altitude risk
- **Cold mornings and icy rim paths.** NWS forecast for the South Rim this week: lows near 28–30°F, highs around 57–60°F, and showers and thunderstorms Friday night into Saturday. Freeze/thaw increases rockfall on trails. Sources: [National Weather Service point forecasts (api.weather.gov, fetched Nov 12)](https://www.weather.gov/); [Grand Canyon Key Hiking Messages (updated Oct 27, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments
- **Hiking down is the easy half.** NPS: rescue is not guaranteed and response can be delayed. If you feel nauseated, dizzy or exhausted, stop, rest, and slowly eat and drink. Don't force fluids; drinking too much can cause hyponatremia. Sources: [Grand Canyon Key Hiking Messages (updated Oct 27, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm); [News release: Select inner canyon trails reopen (Oct 26, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/select-inner-canyon-trails-reopen-in-grand-canyon-national-park.htm)
  - In GOES: Offline interactive health assessment + 50+ visual how-to guides

## Grand Canyon park-wide

- **Water: bring your own.** Stage 4 restrictions are in effect on the South Rim. Inner-canyon water is off everywhere (1.5 Mile and 3 Mile resthouses, Havasupai Gardens) for the rest of the winter. Potable water tanks are at the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trailheads. Carry all your water or a reliable treatment method. Sources: [Grand Canyon Key Hiking Messages (updated Oct 27, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm); [News release: Select inner canyon trails reopen (Oct 26, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/select-inner-canyon-trails-reopen-in-grand-canyon-national-park.htm); [NPS Grand Canyon alerts (NPS Data API, fetched Nov 12)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)
  - In GOES: Offline interactive health assessment + 50+ visual how-to guides
- **Elevation.** The South Rim is about 7,000 ft and the North Rim about 8,000 ft. NPS warns the altitude and dry air can cause dehydration, nausea, shortness of breath and exhaustion, so take your time at overlooks. Sources: [Grand Canyon safety: elevation, rim edges, winter](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/safety.htm)
  - In GOES: High Altitude Illness: personalized altitude risk
- **The rim edge and ice.** Stay on trails and walkways, at least 6 feet from the edge, and never climb railings. In winter, walkways can be icy; NPS recommends over-the-shoe traction and hiking poles. Sources: [Grand Canyon safety: elevation, rim edges, winter](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/safety.htm)
  - In GOES: Offline interactive health assessment + 50+ visual how-to guides
- **Inner canyon hiking.** Temperatures rise about 5.5°F for every 1,000 ft you descend. Rescue isn't guaranteed and response can be delayed. Rockfall is common during or after rain and freeze/thaw, and flash floods can hit with no warning. Sources: [Grand Canyon Key Hiking Messages (updated Oct 27, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm); [News release: Select inner canyon trails reopen (Oct 26, 2026)](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/select-inner-canyon-trails-reopen-in-grand-canyon-national-park.htm)
  - In GOES: Environmental Health: hypothermia and frostbite assessments

## All active Grand Canyon NPS alerts

From the NPS Data API, fetched Nov 12, 2026. Live: https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/conditions.htm

- **[Park Closure] INNER CANYON TRAIL CLOSURES.** Current trail closures: the entire North Kaibab Trail (including Cottonwood Campground), Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, Silver Bridge, and the Black Bridge. Please do not attempt to access the closed areas.
- **[Caution] Overnight Lodging Suspended and Parkwide Stage 4 Water Restrictions.** Grand Canyon National Park has implemented Stage 4 water restrictions on the South Rim due to extensive flash flood damage to the Transcanyon Waterline. Overnight accommodations are suspended, and only dry camping is allowed. The park remains open for day use, with food and beverage services, the Grand Canyon Clinic, and Post Office remaining open. Visitors are asked to bring water with them when visiting the park. (https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/grand-canyon-national-park-to-close-overnight-hotel-accommodations-beginning-august-31.htm)
- **[Danger] INNER CANYON WATER SHUTOFFS.** Beginning Sep. 6, no potable water will be available in the inner canyon, including along the Bright Angel Trail (1.5 Mile Resthouse, 3-Mile Resthouse, Havasupai Gardens day use and campground). All backpackers and hikers should carry water filtration and treatment OR carry all water with them. (https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm)
- **[Caution] Critical Backcountry Updates/Closures.** Visit this link for the inner canyon weather forecast, current trail closures, today's heat risk, and drinking water availability. (https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm)
