AZ · Week of Nov 9–15, 2026

Grand Canyon National Park this week

Day use only on the South Rim: overnight lodging suspended, bring your own water.

This is not a normal November at Grand Canyon. An Aug. 29 flash flood badly damaged the Transcanyon Waterline. The South Rim is under Stage 4 water restrictions, overnight accommodations are suspended (dry camping only), and visitors are asked to bring their own water. The park is open for day use, and food service, the Grand Canyon Clinic and the Post Office remain open. In September NPS projected that pumping could restart around Nov. 16, with phased overnight service around Thanksgiving; it called those dates tentative. NPS alertNPS newsNPS news

Entrances open this week

Pick your entrance

Tusayan, AZ (Hwy 64 / 180)

South Entrance

The South Entrance just north of Tusayan is the main gate into Grand Canyon's South Rim (NPS says it gets the most traffic). It leads to Grand Canyon Village, the main visitor center and the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trailheads.

Open 24/7 — day use, bring water

Cameron, AZ (SR 64 via US 89)

Desert View (East) Entrance

The East Entrance at Desert View is 25 miles west of Cameron on State Route 64, reached via US 89 through the Navajo Nation. It's 32 miles east of Grand Canyon Village, and NPS says waits here are usually shorter than at the South Entrance.

Open 24/7

Jacob Lake, AZ (SR 67)

North Rim Entrance

The North Rim entrance station is on State Route 67, 30 miles south of Jacob Lake. In fall 2026 the North Rim is in modified operations: day use only, with no services. The gate closes for winter at sunset Nov 30 or after the first major snowstorm, whichever comes first.

Limited day use — no services, closing soon

Know the risks

What matters in Grand Canyon right now

Teach, don't scare: these are the hazards NPS emphasizes this season, with what to do.

Water: bring your own

Stage 4 restrictions are in effect on the South Rim. Inner-canyon water is off everywhere (1.5 Mile and 3 Mile resthouses, Havasupai Gardens) for the rest of the winter. Potable water tanks are at the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trailheads. Carry all your water or a reliable treatment method. NPSNPS newsNPS alert

Elevation

The South Rim is about 7,000 ft and the North Rim about 8,000 ft. NPS warns the altitude and dry air can cause dehydration, nausea, shortness of breath and exhaustion, so take your time at overlooks. NPS

The rim edge and ice

Stay on trails and walkways, at least 6 feet from the edge, and never climb railings. In winter, walkways can be icy; NPS recommends over-the-shoe traction and hiking poles. NPS

Inner canyon hiking

Temperatures rise about 5.5°F for every 1,000 ft you descend. Rescue isn't guaranteed and response can be delayed. Rockfall is common during or after rain and freeze/thaw, and flash floods can hit with no warning. NPSNPS news
