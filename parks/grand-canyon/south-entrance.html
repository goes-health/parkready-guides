Grand Canyon South Entrance: this week

Open 24/7 — day use, bring water Week of Nov 9–15, 2026

The South Entrance just north of Tusayan is the main gate into Grand Canyon's South Rim (NPS says it gets the most traffic). It leads to Grand Canyon Village, the main visitor center and the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trailheads.

Know the risks

What actually matters here in mid-November

The goal is a good trip, not a scary one. These are the hazards NPS highlights for this area and season, and what to do about each.

Dehydration in high, dry air

At 7,000 feet in desert air you lose water fast, and the park is asking everyone to bring their own. Inner-canyon water stations are off. NPSNPS

Cold mornings and icy rim paths

NWS forecast for the South Rim this week: lows near 28–30°F, highs around 57–60°F, and showers and thunderstorms Friday night into Saturday. Freeze/thaw increases rockfall on trails. NWSNPS

Hiking down is the easy half

NPS: rescue is not guaranteed and response can be delayed. If you feel nauseated, dizzy or exhausted, stop, rest, and slowly eat and drink. Don't force fluids; drinking too much can cause hyponatremia. NPSNPS news

Park-wide

Grand Canyon risks everywhere in the park

Water: bring your own

Stage 4 restrictions are in effect on the South Rim. Inner-canyon water is off everywhere (1.5 Mile and 3 Mile resthouses, Havasupai Gardens) for the rest of the winter. Potable water tanks are at the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trailheads. Carry all your water or a reliable treatment method. NPSNPS newsNPS alert

Elevation

The South Rim is about 7,000 ft and the North Rim about 8,000 ft. NPS warns the altitude and dry air can cause dehydration, nausea, shortness of breath and exhaustion, so take your time at overlooks. NPS

In GOESHigh Altitude Illness: personalized altitude risk

The rim edge and ice

Stay on trails and walkways, at least 6 feet from the edge, and never climb railings. In winter, walkways can be icy; NPS recommends over-the-shoe traction and hiking poles. NPS

Inner canyon hiking

Temperatures rise about 5.5°F for every 1,000 ft you descend. Rescue isn't guaranteed and response can be delayed. Rockfall is common during or after rain and freeze/thaw, and flash floods can hit with no warning. NPSNPS news
